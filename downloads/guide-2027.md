## Start with identity, then choose the finish

A cleaner background cannot compensate for a changed setting. Before comparing polish, check stone count and placement, the outline, prongs, chain links, closures, engraving, and material appearance. A result can look attractive while showing a different product.

Separate two questions: does this image represent this item, and does it suit this destination? Manufacturers need collection views tied to model codes. Wholesale buyers need to compare a selection. Retailers need consistent framing alongside accurate detail views. Photographers may need an editable first pass followed by individual finishing. Ecommerce is one destination among catalogs, line sheets, trade presentations, social campaigns, and sales conversations.

Write the requirements before trying tools. Otherwise, a dramatic example can distract from the construction details your customer will rely on. Decide which details must be visible, what your source establishes, and who will approve the final file.

## Build a small real-collection test

Choose a manageable group exposing different risks: reflective gold, a fine chain or openwork piece, visible prongs, a matched pair, and engraving if your range includes it. Add a typical everyday item so you do not judge the process only on difficult exceptions. This is a practical selection, not a statistically representative benchmark.

Keep original files unchanged. Record the item code, destination, requested treatment, reference, and output version. Give candidate workflows the same task where their input requirements permit. Do not compare a studio's best new capture against a weak phone photograph and attribute the whole difference to software.

Inspect full-size exports beside the source and, where available, the physical piece or a trusted reference. Reject identity changes first. Then assess edges, reflections, background, framing, and readability at the intended size. Record whether each result needs another version, manual finishing, or a reshoot. Keep rejected outputs: they explain where the production boundary belongs.

## Decide by trade workflow

**Manufacturers and brands:** test consistency without losing item distinctions. Establish a crop and background brief, then check that shared treatment has not homogenized different designs. Keep model codes and approvals attached through export. A successfully processed batch still requires individual decisions.

**Wholesalers and exporters:** build buyer selections from approved images and explicit identifiers. A line sheet should make the range easier to compare. Check that crops do not exaggerate relative size; add verified dimensions when comparison requires them. Never infer a measurement from generated imagery.

**Retailers and resellers:** preserve the specific item being offered. For an individual used or vintage piece, editing should not hide condition information needed by the buyer. Pair a clean presentation view with original detail photography where the selling context requires it.

**Photographers and retouchers:** check whether a generative pass removes useful work or creates detail repairs. Define which tasks remain in your established editor and who signs off the file.
